18. Vendor costs: monitoring, alerts and stop points (Daniel, Nov 16)
Daniel, Nov 16, PDS thread: "you should create a rule that all vendors that need payment has cost monitoring across all features."
1. Scope. Every vendor that bills us or can start billing us (a card on file, a paid plan, or a free plan with paid overage), for every feature that uses it. That includes vendors we already pay: a feature that adds usage on one states that vendor's controls too, or says in one line why the feature cannot add cost there.
2. Before a feature uses such a vendor, four things are in place and written down: the plan and its limits; the expected cost per month; an alert to Daniel, with its amount, which charges it covers and how late it reports; and a stop point. An alert is a warning, not a bound: vendors report usage late, so spending can pass the alert amount before the mail arrives. Only a stop point bounds spending. The stop point is the vendor's own hard cap where it has one and stopping there is safe. Where the vendor has none, or where its cap would take production offline, the stop point is a guard in our own code, or a stated reason why there is none. Where there is no stop point, the proposal says so in those words and gives an exposure estimate: the usage it assumes, the vendor's price for it, and the resulting amount per month. An estimate is not a bound, and the proposal says what, if anything, enforces it. Where no defensible estimate can be made, the proposal says "no finite maximum established". Daniel accepts the estimate, or that statement, by name in the proposal.
3. Free tools first: the vendor's own budget alerts, usage limits and free-plan caps. Paying for a cost-monitoring product needs its own proposal.
4. Moving to a paid plan, turning on paid overage, or raising a cap or an alert amount: Daniel, his explicit word naming it. Billing settings are his to change in the vendor's web UI; a thread gives him the page and the exact values and never changes them itself.
5. Proposals. The "Running cost" row of every feature proposal (section 16) states, for each vendor the feature touches: the plan, the expected monthly cost, the alert (amount, coverage, delay), the stop point, or the exposure estimate or "no finite maximum established" for Daniel to accept, and what users see at the stop point. A packet without these is not sent.
6. Register. One line per vendor: plan, card on file or not, alert and amount, stop point, features using it, date last checked. A thread that adds a vendor, or a feature on a vendor, updates the line in the same step. The register is the Sheet "Iriai — Vendor cost register" in Iriai/Iriai Eng (https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1dPE60YFmnighhp4HjFTSVzZ4vGkOHRrVAMGxRXmAyzM/edit), created Nov 16; values only Daniel can read are marked [Daniel].
7. Facts as of Nov 16, from the vendors' own documentation. Railway: a free email alert and a free hard limit per workspace; the hard limit takes every workload offline, so production uses the email alert and an exposure estimate Daniel accepts (item 2) unless he decides otherwise. Cloudflare: budget alerts are free, account-wide and email only; they are worked out from the previous day's usage, arrive after the amount is passed, leave out subscription charges, and do not stop usage. Resend: the free plan refuses mail past its quota and bills nothing; paid overage is off unless an admin turns it on.
